## 2. “Silent fix” is definition-sensitive

The existing figure-generation code searches only `title + description`, using
the case-sensitive pattern `CVE-|GHSA-`. This produces 109 records (4.9%).
Rated severity is a different signal and covers 143 records (6.4%).

| Operational definition | Records with neither signal | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| No rating and no narrow `CVE-/GHSA-` title/description match | 1,991 | 89.5% |
| No rating and no recognized CVE/GHSA/RustSec ID in any provenance field | 1,962 | 88.2% |

The broader definition searches `title`, `description`, `issue_id`, `contest`,
`source_url`, and `evidence` case-insensitively. It finds 172 records (7.7%)
with a recognized advisory identifier.

**Do not claim yet.** “93.6% are silent (no advisory and no rating)” is not
supported by the current snapshot. The 93.6% figure is exactly the *unrated*
share, not the intersection of “unrated” and “no advisory.” Depending on the
advisory definition, the supported descriptive estimate is 88.2–89.5%.

**Interpretation.** The central “beyond CVEs” thesis remains strong, but the
paper must publish its advisory-linkage definition and sensitivity range rather
than equating “unrated” with “silent.”

## 3. `cwe_top25` is a sparse general CWE field

| Measure | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Any non-`N/A` CWE | 396 | 17.8% of all records |
| Member of MITRE 2025 CWE Top 25 | 130 | 5.8% of all records |
| Top-25 member among CWE-known records | 130 | 32.8% of CWE-known records |
| CWE-known but outside the 2025 Top 25 | 266 | 67.2% of CWE-known records |

The most frequent assigned values are CWE-248 (60), CWE-20 (50), CWE-362
(48), CWE-400 (42), and CWE-476 (33). Only CWE-20 and CWE-476 in that top five
belong to the 2025 Top 25.

**Observation.** The column name `cwe_top25` is misleading: 266 of its 396
non-empty values are not in the 2025 CWE Top 25. Documentation reporting 24.7%
coverage belongs to the earlier build; the frozen snapshot has 17.8% coverage.

**Do not claim yet.** The 5.8% Top-25 coverage cannot establish domain shift,
because 82.2% of rows have no CWE assignment. A publication-grade comparison
requires a stratified, manually reviewed mapping sample.

## 4. Severity provenance currently overloads `upstream-cvss`

The snapshot contains 612 rows whose `severity_source` is `upstream-cvss`:

| Severity state | Count | Share of `upstream-cvss` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rated Critical/High/Medium/Low | 83 | 13.6% |
| Info or Unrated | 529 | 86.4% |

The methodology describes approximately 83 upstream dependency CVEs. The code
also classifies every changelog or release URL as a dependency, which explains
why the stored provenance expands to 612 rows even though only 83 carry a rated
upstream severity.

**Do not claim yet.** `severity_source == upstream-cvss` cannot currently be
used as a dependency-CVE ground-truth flag. The dependency classifier and the
meaning of `severity_source` must be separated before CVSS-versus-bounty
analysis.

The leading attack-path labels are malformed input (926; 41.6%), crafted state
(415; 18.7%), malicious P2P message (241; 10.8%), and internal-only paths
(195; 8.8%).

**Hypothesis.** Ethereum-client fixes are more availability- and
determinism-centred than a general CVE corpus. This is not yet a result: the
labels are partly heuristic/LLM-derived, and the external corpus must be mapped
to a common taxonomy.

## 6. Decisions for the next analysis

1. Treat the 2,225-row Parquet file as the frozen descriptive snapshot.
2. Report both narrow and broad advisory-linkage definitions.
3. Run every primary comparison on A-only, A∪B, and A∪B∪C populations.
4. Do not use `severity_source=upstream-cvss` as a dependency flag until its
   broad URL heuristic is corrected or independently audited.
5. Call `cwe_top25` a “CWE label” in prose; separately test membership in a
   versioned MITRE Top 25 list.
6. Reserve causal or generalizing language for manually validated labels and
   statistical tests.
